Goal observations can include state variables that a planning task does not require the agent to match. I examine
this mismatch in the released JEPA-WM planner on PushT: its proprioceptive goal cost penalises the expert's goal-frame
velocity, although the endpoint success criterion ignores velocity. Using the model's linear encoder, I decompose the
cost and construct VELDROP-z, which retains the decoded position energy and standardises it against the visual term.
The correction requires no retraining or outcome labels at inference. On a fresh cohort of \RTwN{} starts,
reduced-budget cross-entropy search with VELDROP-z lowered mean executed margin from \RTwRawMargin{} to
\RTwVZMargin{} (paired difference \RTwQTwoD{} [\RTwQTwoLo, \RTwQTwoHi]) and increased successes from
\RTwRawSucc{} to \RTwVZSucc{} with no losses, repeating an earlier result on \RNineN{} goals. The pre-specified primary
comparison between position and full proprioception at a shared frozen weight remained uncertain:
\RTwQOneD{} [\RTwQOneLo, \RTwQOneHi]. An additional arm, designed after analysis of the first four arms,
standardised the full proprioceptive term and achieved \RThSucc{} successes. VELDROP-z added \RThGain{} successes
relative to this arm, but its margin difference was uncertain (\RThD{} [\RThLo, \RThHi]). This exploratory comparison
changes both the retained component and its adaptive weighting, so it does not isolate a velocity-only effect.
Evaluation is limited to released checkpoints, training-split goals, a reduced search budget and open-loop execution.
The evidence supports a scoped planning-cost correction and identifies the evaluations needed to establish its
benefit beyond this setting.
